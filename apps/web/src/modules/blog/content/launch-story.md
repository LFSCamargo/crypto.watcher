# We're building the Moody's of crypto — and we're ready to launch

For a century, capital markets have relied on **classification houses** — S&P Global Ratings, Moody's, Fitch — to translate complexity into trust. When a municipality issues bonds or a corporation raises debt, investors don't guess. They read an independent assessment from specialists who stake their reputation on every letter grade.

Crypto never got that layer.

Instead, we got star ratings from anonymous crowds, influencer threads, and vote-to-rate platforms where influence can be bought. Popularity became a proxy for safety. Scams wore the same UI as protocols. And when things collapsed, the question was always the same: *who was responsible for telling us this was risky?*

**Crypto Watcher exists to answer that question.**

## What we learned from the rating agencies

S&P, Moody's and Fitch are not perfect — they have blind spots, conflicts, and critics. But they established a model that still matters:

- **Independent specialists** publish structured assessments, not vibes.
- **Methodology is explicit** — you can disagree with a grade, but you can read *why* it was assigned.
- **Accountability exists** — analysts and institutions stand behind their work.
- **The public consumes; experts decide** — markets don't let every shareholder vote on credit ratings.

We are applying that same discipline to blockchain projects, protocols and assets — adapted for open networks, on-chain evidence, and a community that can submit findings without corrupting the final score.

## Expertise, not popularity

Crypto Watcher is an **expert-governed** risk analysis platform. Official scores and structured reports are published only by a **vetted governor council** with demonstrated knowledge in:

- Smart contract security
- Protocol design and tokenomics
- Compliance and regulatory exposure
- Infrastructure and operational resilience
- On-chain analysis

Regular users **do not vote on the final risk score**. You can browse reports, comment, submit evidence, and report incidents — but score authority stays with governors who are selected, accountable, and transparent about conflicts of interest.

That is intentional. We reject vote-to-rate systems where project ratings can be purchased or brigaded. Trust must come from **reviewer expertise**, not token ownership.

## How a review works

Every project follows the same lifecycle:

1. **Review requested** — a team or user pays to enter the queue.
2. **In analysis** — governors investigate; the community submits evidence.
3. **Governor review** — findings are synthesized into a structured report.
4. **Report published** — the official score is shown to everyone.

Published outcomes can be anchored **on-chain** for auditability. The governance token (CWT) governs **protocol** decisions — reviewer roster, methodology, fees — not individual project grades.
